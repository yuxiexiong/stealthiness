## 0. Least favourable items first

1. **A crossover of "data-order seed vs init seed" with a behaviour-specific readout already exists outside the backdoor literature**: Ganesh, Chang, Strobel, Shokri, FAccT'23 (arXiv 2307.04138, a citer of Shumailov). They fix reshuffling and vary init, fix init and vary reshuffling, and report that reshuffling carries the run-to-run variance of a subgroup-level behaviour (correlation across runs .94 with fixed reshuffling vs .04 with fixed init on ACSIncome). Their dependent variable is an endpoint/at-epoch fairness gap, not an event time, and no subset of the data has its positions pinned. It also brings a competing explanation for our fill effect: **recency** (they show the effect is carried by the most recent ~b batches).
2. **Order-only manipulation with an event-time readout exists on a toy task**: LeDoux, arXiv 2603.25047 ("The Order Is The Message", a reference of 2606.29554). Same data, same everything, only example order varies; readout is the epoch at which test accuracy reaches 99.5% (487 / 659 / never). It orders the whole training set (no target / non-target split), and its seeds change init and the data sample together.
3. **On the same object class (LLM backdoor fine-tuning, poison set held fixed), a Sept-2026 paper reports that mini-batch stochasticity alone decides the endpoint**: Muhamed et al., arXiv 2609.15029 App. F.9: full-batch 0% ASR vs "up to 71% ASR on individual seeds" under mini-batch SGD on the same training data. HOW-MUCH axis only; the seed is not decomposed into order vs init; no timing readout.
4. **A 761-run fine-tuning study reports that order effects on endpoint capability sit at the run-to-run floor and that winners do not survive re-running**: arXiv 2609.26160 (citer of both 2606.29554 and 2601.21698). It also reports a negative result for the optimiser-memory and batch-composition readings in its setting. Endpoint axis, block/interleave lever.
5. **What I did NOT find** (lower bound, see §7): among 85 full texts of arXiv-indexed citers of Souly (Oct 2025 – Nov 2026) there are 0 occurrences of "path dependen(ce/t)", and no experiment that varies poison placement or clean-data order as a follow-up to App. F.2. No paper in the whole chain uses the design "target-example schedule fixed (identity + positions), only non-target order permuted, readout = step of acquisition". This is an absence in an incomplete index, not a proof of absence.

**What was varied / held fixed in F.1–F.4 (my reading of the text above):**
- Varied: (a) the *placement of the poisoned block* — three levels: uniform / "randomly shuffled", beginning, end; in the "beginning" arm the block is "the uniformly mixed poisoned harmful and clean harmful data", followed by the non-harmful data; (b) number of poisoned samples; (c) total dataset size (1000 vs 10000; 100k for the continued-clean-FT panel); (d) constant LR value (F.3); (e) constant vs linearly decreasing LR (F.4).
- Readout: end-of-run ASR (plus NTA, CA); Fig. 17b tracks ASR against "Fine-tuning Step After Poisoning". No step-of-acquisition statistic is defined or reported.
- **Was the order of clean samples ever varied with poison positions fixed? No.** No arm fixes poison positions and re-permutes the clean data. In the "uniform" arm the poison positions themselves are re-randomised.
- **Were shuffle seeds separated from init seeds? Not stated, and nothing indicates it.** The word "seed" does not occur in App. E or F. Each point is "the median of 5 experiments"; the text does not say what differs among the 5 (App. E says clean and poisoned samples are randomly sampled "for each experiment", so the 5 may differ in data identity as well as order). Only medians are highlighted; no spread statistic is defined. Whether fine-tuning is full or adapter-based, and hence whether any random init exists, is not stated in §5.1 / App. E–F.
- Authors' own attribution for the end-placement failure under the schedule is the learning rate ("This is due to the small learning rate at the end of the fine-tuning", F.4); for the constant-LR beginning-vs-end asymmetry it is an unexplained hypothesis ("We do not have a good explanation…", F.2).
